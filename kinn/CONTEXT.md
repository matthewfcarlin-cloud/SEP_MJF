# Kinn (now RECO) — Product Context

> **Rebrand (Oct 26, 2026):** the product and app are now **RECO**. This file keeps the Kinn name in its history. App and docs: [`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md).

_Last updated: October 14, 2026 · Owner: Matthew Carlin (USC Iovine and Young Academy)_

This file holds the product context: what Kinn is, why it exists, how it works, what has been decided and what's still open. It's meant to be the starting point for anyone (or any AI assistant) picking up the project.

---

## 1. One-line summary

**Kinn is thin kinesiology tape with printed stretch sensors and a reusable snap-in "coin." It measures how a joint actually moves during recovery, so patients and physical therapists can see progress between visits instead of guessing.**

Working tagline on the site: **"Tape that tracks your recovery."**
Alternates: "Recovery you can measure." · "Know your range."
Rejected: "One coin. Every movement." (Matthew didn't like it.)

## 2. How it started (Poise → Kinn)

| Stage | What it was | Why it changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Poise** (first concept) | A thin posture strip stuck across the shoulder blades. It detected shoulder rounding by stretch and buzzed or notified you. It was an "invisible" fix to a problem Matthew has himself. | The sensing tech (stretch traces + a small BLE coin) is general. Posture alone is a crowded, low-willingness-to-pay market (Upright Go etc.). |
| **Kinn** (current) | A general **physical therapy and movement-tracking platform**. One reusable coin snaps into **replaceable tape strips shaped for different joints**. The coin is an ecosystem piece that could later be used on other products. | PT has a real, documented pain point (home exercise adherence) and a payer path (remote therapeutic monitoring codes). |

The Poise demo, renders and early Fusion CAD are in [`archive/poise/`](archive/poise/).

## 3. The problem

- A PT typically sees a patient about **one hour a week**. The other ~167 hours, when most of the recovery work happens, are unmeasured.
- About **76% of patients don't fully stick to their home exercise program** (Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center survey of PT patients). Patients forget, lose motivation, or don't know if they're doing exercises right.
- Therapists have little objective data on range of motion or adherence between visits. Most existing tools need a camera (patient films themselves) or bulky hardware.

## 4. The product

### 4.1 System
1. **Strip (disposable):** kinesiology tape with **conductive silver ink printed directly on the weave** in a serpentine (zigzag) pattern, so the traces stretch with the tape instead of cracking. The strip has a low dock pad with **3 gold contacts** where the coin connects. The back has a peel-away liner like normal KT tape.
2. **Coin (reusable):** about 22 mm across, a domed white ceramic-look top with a **teal LED status ring**. It contains the Bluetooth radio, a motion sensor (IMU) and a rechargeable battery. It snaps into any strip's dock. **All the expensive parts live in the coin; the tape is the refill.**
3. **App:** turns stretch and motion readings into bend angle, reps, hold time, streaks and trends. Optional sharing with the PT (clinic view).

### 4.2 Strip family (all share the same dock)
| Strip | Shape | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Knee** | Body + 4 tails + oval window around the kneecap; traces loop the patella | Bend angle, reps |
| **Shoulder** | Y-strip | Arm raise, rotation |
| **Elbow** | Smaller Y | Flex / extend |
| **Back** | Straight I-strip (where the Poise idea started) | Posture, bending |

Shape geometry (mm) is generated by [`blender/gen_shapes.py`](blender/gen_shapes.py) → [`blender/shapes.json`](blender/shapes.json).

### 4.3 How sensing works (be careful with claims)
- Printed serpentine traces change **resistance/capacitance when stretched**, and joint bending stretches the tape. The coin reads that change.
- The coin's **IMU** adds orientation and motion (rep counting, speed).
- Together these give **range of motion, reps, hold time and adherence** for one joint.
- ⚠️ **Not** claimed: detailed sports form analysis from capacitance alone. A single stretch channel can't see full-body form. Matthew flagged this explicitly; don't overclaim.
- ⚠️ Not FDA-cleared. Copy must say it **tracks/shows**, never **diagnoses/treats**.

### 4.4 Prototype plan
- **Coin prototype:** Seeed XIAO nRF52840 Sense (BLE + built-in IMU).
- **Sensor:** the Amazon ZD10-100 Matthew found is an **FSR (force/pressure) sensor, not a bend sensor**. For flex, use a Spectra Symbol-style flex sensor, or conductive stretch fabric/ink, integrated into athletic tape.
- More sensors in the coin can come later.

### 4.5 Industrial design decisions
- ✅ Must read clearly as **tape**: thin, athletic, like KT Tape (reference: black KT Y-strip with the logo printed near the tail).
- ✅ **Exposed / printed traces** are preferred (visible silver zigzags are part of the look).
- ✅ Strips must be **replaceable and cheap to make** (printing ink on tape is the cheap path).
- ✅ Clean, minimal, high-quality.
- ❌ **No stitching**, no pillowed center spine (Concept B, rejected).
- Colors explored: sand (main), black, slate.

## 5. Market notes (from earlier research; verify before quoting)

- **Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM)** CPT codes let US clinics bill for monitoring between visits. Example: **98977** (musculoskeletal device supply) was about **$47 per patient per month** and needs **16+ days of data in 30 days**. Codes 98975–98981 cover setup, device supply and management time.
- **Competitors / adjacent products:**
  - **Hinge Health:** digital MSK program; TrueMotion uses the phone camera.
  - **Sword Health:** digital PT with motion sensors.
  - **TracPatch:** knee-specific wearable patch (post-op).
  - **Upright Go:** posture tilt sensor.
  - **Kinetic REFLEX:** wearable posture and lifting sensor for workplaces.
- **Differentiation:** tape people already accept, no camera, a cheap disposable sensor, and one coin across many joints.

## 10. Current focus (Oct 16, 2026): the "know your limit" alert

**Most promising use case (from team meeting):**
- **Safe-range alert:** an all-day strip that buzzes your phone when you bend an injured joint past a limit. People in rehab want to keep moving and strengthening, but don't know where productive rehab ends and re-injury starts. One teammate's current shoulder injury was the example. Turning that line into actual numbers would be new, and nothing readily available does it.
- **Wear-all-day mode:** count how much the injured joint is used across the whole day, not just during PT exercises.

**Open questions for interviews (not yet answered):**
- How is the safe limit set? The PT, the stage of recovery, the patient's own history?
- Can stretch/angle measurements actually indicate re-injury risk, or only range?
- Do patients and PTs want this feedback, and how often before it gets annoying?

**Site changes for this focus:**
- Hero captions: "Rehab means moving an injury without knowing where the line is." and "Push your recovery, not your injury."
- Mission heading: "KNOW YOUR LIMIT."
- "How it works" is a rendered sequence (Oct 17, replaced an abstract canvas demo): the real knee strip slowly bends in a black void, scrubbed by scroll. Woven fibers and printed silver serpentine traces are visible; the traces flatten and stretch through the bend, signal pulses run toward the coin, and the coin's LED ring lights when flexion passes the line. A flexion readout and gauge (line at 120°) track the frames, and the phone-style alert appears past the line. The four steps (Wear it all day / Set your line / Move normally / Get a nudge) fill in white as you scroll, instead of cycling on a timer. Source: `blender/kinn_flex.py` (runs headless with `pip install bpy`), frames in `site/public/flex/`.
- The intake now also asks "Would a buzz when you push an injury past a safe range help you?" (yes/maybe/no, stored as `alert`) and adds shoulder, ankle and other joints to the injury list.

## 11. Direction update (Oct 17, 2026): "Trust your comeback"

- **Who it's for:** anyone coming back from an injury, not just PT patients. The first story is athletes returning after ACL surgery. Matthew wants the app itself to give people the confidence to exercise, or to rest, without relying on a PT to set things up.
- **Core problem:** people are healthy enough to try but not sure how hard to push. Holding back drags recovery out, and pushing too hard causes setbacks.
- **Where the AI fits (Matthew's framing):**
  - Validating movements: did each rep hit full range and move the way it should?
  - Tracking flare-ups and pain: a one-tap log, lined up with strip data to spot what triggers flare-ups.
  - Learning the person's normal: a baseline that gets sharper with every day of wear, used to say push, ease off, or rest.
  - **Goal:** a shorter recovery and fewer setbacks. Claim this as a goal only; "not a medical device".
- **Pitch angle (Continual Learning track):** the model learns from data only the strip captures (all-day bend, load, range), so its observations get more specific over time. Not just a journaling app on top of a language model.
- **Site v3.1 changes:**
  - Tagline: "Trust your comeback".
  - New hero captions.
  - The mission scroll text now reads "TRUST YOUR COMEBACK."
  - Flex steps: Wear it all day / Check every rep / Log how it feels / Know when to push. The alert now references your last flare-up.
  - New "It learns your recovery" section.
  - Intake question changed to "Would an app that tells you when to push and when to rest help your recovery?" (still stored as `alert`).
- A "Built like tape" section with Matthew's generated product photos was tried and removed (he didn't like them).
- **The redesign doc** (paper/midnight/lime, hardware hidden) was tried and rejected. It's kept in `archive/website-v4/`.
- **To validate:** what would actually make someone confident enough to push after an injury?

## 12. AI demo for the hackathon (Oct 17, 2026): "Every Kinn gets smarter"

- **Where:** a page on the Kinn site at `/learn` (linked from the header, the "It learns your recovery" section and the footer). Source in [`learn/`](learn/) (Vite + React), built into `site/public/learn/`; the site's server reads typed check-ins with Claude (`site/checkin.js`, needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in the Vercel project). Its README has the demo script and the numbers.
- **Prompt:** Continual Learning. Two loops: each phone learns from its wearer's evening check-ins (online learning), and every night the phones pool what they learned by federated averaging (only model weights leave the phone), so new wearers start smarter. Second track: Design. Scale is a pitch angle (data network effect).
- **Sensors claimed:** stretch traces (bend), motion (deep bends), a muscle sensor over the quad for muscle fatigue (the KineTape prototype's thin-film pressure sensor), and skin temperature. Still "not a medical device"; it estimates flare-up risk, not tissue strain.
- **App simplified** from KineTape's 8 tabs to 3: Today (one call: Push / Ease off / Rest, plus your line), Check in (one tap or a sentence Claude reads), Learned (what warns of a flare-up for you, and a timeline).
- All wearers and readings are simulated; the learning math is real.

## 13. App spec and decisions (Oct 19, 2026)

- **ICP narrowed:** pro and college athletes recovering from ACL reconstruction, weeks 4 to 24, who see a physio 1 to 2× a week.
- **Core feature:** a daily 0 to 100 knee **Load score** against a stage-based target band, with a plain-language call (Room to push / On target / Ease off) and a reason sentence. Range alert, Moments (coin button), flare-up insights and an athlete-controlled physio report support it. Never adherence or compliance language.
- **Documents:** [`docs/feature-spec.md`](docs/feature-spec.md) (features, load model, physio questions, FDA wording), [`docs/architecture.md`](docs/architecture.md) (Expo + TypeScript core, data model, sensor abstraction), [`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md) (running decision log), [`docs/scoring-methods.md`](docs/scoring-methods.md) (how Recovery % and Knee load are computed, with evidence tags), [`docs/backend.md`](docs/backend.md) (how the app derives everything on the phone, the tape client, the site API).
- **Interview evidence so far:** five Granola-recorded calls, Oct 15 to 19 (sarena, Akshay, Lowie/Ava/Sammie, paxton, Riya). All athletes or friends; no physio yet.
