Stick it on. Snap it in. Move.

No camera, no brace, no app to set up at the clinic. Just tape you'd wear anyway, with the sensor printed in.

01Stick

Pick the strip cut for your joint. Peel the liner and apply it like any kinesiology tape.

02Snap

The coin clicks into the dock and connects to the printed traces through three gold contacts.

03Move

Do your exercises. As the joint bends, the traces stretch. The coin reads that change plus its own motion sensor.

04 See

Your phone shows today's range, your reps and your streak. If you choose, your PT sees it too.

Not a brace. Not a camera.

Printed sensors you can't feel

Conductive ink is printed straight onto the tape in a zigzag, so it flexes with your skin. No wires, no stitching, no bulk under your clothes.

The coin moves. The tape is the refill.

Everything expensive lives in the 22 mm coin: Bluetooth, a motion sensor and a rechargeable battery. It pops out of a used strip and into a fresh one in a second.

Questions

Is Kinn a medical device?

Not yet. Kinn is in development and has not been cleared by the FDA. It tracks movement. It doesn't diagnose or treat anything, and it doesn't replace your physical therapist.

How long does a strip last?

We're designing strips to be worn for several days, like regular kinesiology tape. Then you peel it off, snap the coin out, and put on a fresh one.

Do I need a physical therapist to use it?

No. You can use Kinn on your own to track your exercises. Sharing your data with a PT is optional.

Will it work with my phone?

The coin connects over Bluetooth. We're building for iPhone and Android.

When does it ship, and what will it cost?

We're building the first batch now. Everyone on the waitlist hears the price and date first, and reserving costs nothing.
